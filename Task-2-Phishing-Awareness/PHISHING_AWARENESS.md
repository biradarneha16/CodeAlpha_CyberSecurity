# Phishing Awareness Training Module

## What is phishing?
Phishing is social engineering designed to trick a person into revealing information, opening harmful content, transferring money, or taking another unsafe action.

## Recognizing phishing emails
Check for unexpected requests, sender/domain mismatch, look-alike domains, urgency or fear, unexpected attachments, suspicious links, requests for passwords or one-time codes, and unusual wording or workflow.

## Fake websites
Check the complete domain; watch for look-alike spellings and unexpected subdomains; do not treat HTTPS alone as proof of legitimacy; use known bookmarks or manually entered official addresses for important services; use a password manager where appropriate.

## Social engineering
Common tactics include authority, urgency, fear, curiosity, reciprocity, and familiarity.

## Best practices
Pause before clicking. Verify unexpected requests through a separate trusted channel. Never disclose passwords or one-time codes because a message asks. Use MFA and unique passwords. Keep software updated. Report suspected phishing. If you interact with suspicious content, report promptly and follow incident-response procedures.

## Safe scenario
A message says a shared document will be deleted in 30 minutes and asks the employee to sign in immediately. Warning signs are the unexpected deadline, pressure, and login request. Do not use the message's login button; open the normal portal from a trusted bookmark or verify through another channel.

## Quick checklist
**STOP → CHECK → VERIFY → REPORT**
